Before You Begin

Drive the Trans-Canada west from the Manitoba line and, a little past Moosomin, a grain elevator rises out of the fields. That is Wapella. Today it is a quiet town of a few hundred people. A hundred and forty years ago it was one of the first stops the railway made in what was then the District of Assiniboia, and the people who got off the train there came from a remarkable range of places: the Hebridean islands off the west coast of Scotland, the towns and villages of England, and the Jewish communities of Bessarabia and southern Russia, with many more to follow.

This book tells their story. It follows the land from the signing of Treaty 4 in 1874, through the coming of the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1882, the Scottish crofters of 1883, the Jewish farm colony that began in 1886 and became the first successful one in Canada, the young family named Bronfman who spent their first prairie years nearby, and the town that grew up around the station: its newspaper, its hotels, its handsome brick town hall, its ball team of 1924 and its hockey champions.

It is built the way everything on TownTales is built. Each fact comes from a source you can check, and the sources are listed at the back. When two sources disagree about a date, both are given. When a story rests on a single report, the book says so. Nothing has been made up to fill a gap.

The last part of the book is a Roll of Remembrance, the names of more than thirteen hundred people buried in the cemeteries of the Wapella district, as recorded by volunteer transcribers. Many readers will turn to it first. It is the reason this book exists: a town is its people, and every name on that list had a story.

Some of those stories are still missing. If you have a photograph, a letter, a newspaper clipping or a memory that belongs in the next edition, share it at towntales.ca. Every copy of this book is printed when it is ordered, so the history inside it keeps growing.
