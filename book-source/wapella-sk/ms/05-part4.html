The Cemeteries

Six burial grounds hold the people of the Wapella district. Each one tells part of the story in this book, and together they are its most complete record.

Wapella Community Cemetery

Just east of town on the old Number 1 Highway, on the northeast quarter of section 9, township 15, range 33, west of the first meridian, lies the Wapella Community Cemetery. It is non-denominational and still in use, cared for by the Town of Wapella. More than 1,100 people are recorded here, from pioneers born in the early 1800s to residents who died in the last few years. The names reflect every wave of settlement: Scottish and English names from the 1880s, names such as Steinke, Restau, Beutler, Ikert and Schoepp that appear in the decades that followed, and a man named Wong Pong, born in 1877 and died in 1971, whose story has not yet been told.

St. Paul’s Cemetery

About nine kilometres south of Wapella, on SW 18-14-33 W1, St. Paul’s Cemetery served the Scottish settlement. Its congregation had Presbyterian roots and later belonged to the United Church. Its first burial was in 1883. The 163 names recorded here are among the oldest in the district, and many stones give a person’s age at death rather than a birth year, as was the custom: a boy of twelve, a child of nineteen days, a woman of ninety. It is the resting place of Rev. D. F. McMillan, who died in 1916.

St. Andrew’s Cemetery

About eleven kilometres south of Wapella, on NE 31-13-33 W1, stand St. Andrew’s Roman Catholic Church, its hall and its cemetery, still active. The church, hall and cemetery together are a designated Municipal Heritage Property of the RM of Martin.

Earlswood Cemetery

About eighteen kilometres south of Wapella, near Red Jacket on NW 13-13-34 W1, the Earlswood Cemetery and Chapel served Presbyterian and later United Church families. It is also a designated Municipal Heritage Property of the RM of Martin. Among its earliest recorded burials are Mary A. Graham, who died on New Year’s Day 1887 at thirty-three, and Angus Graham, who died in 1889.

Buck Cemetery

Northwest of Wapella, on NW 12-15-32 W1, is the Buck Cemetery, a small private family burial ground, now closed.

Wapella Jewish Cemetery

Somewhere in the old colony lands north of Wapella is the Wapella Jewish Cemetery, on land donated by Abraham Klenman. Its precise location has not been published in the sources found for this book, and no transcription of its stones has yet come to light. If you know it, or have photographs of its markers, please share them at towntales.ca so its people can be added to the next edition.

How to read the Roll

The Roll of Remembrance that follows lists every name TownTales has recorded for the cemeteries of the Wapella district: 1,304 people in all. Names and dates are given exactly as the cemetery transcriptions record them. A woman’s maiden name, where it is known, appears in brackets. Some stones give only a year, some a full date, and some only the age at death. A few names have no dates at all.

Spellings are as recorded, even where they differ between family members. If you find a mistake, or someone who is missing, please tell us at towntales.ca and we will correct it in the next printing.
